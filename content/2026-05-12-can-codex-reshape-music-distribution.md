## English translation

After weighing costs and returns, I started thinking about how Codex might be applied to my own field: music distribution and promotion.

After thinking through the costs and returns, I naturally started wondering how I could run the same Codex experiment in my own professional field: music distribution and promotion.

Once the idea appeared, I immediately noticed many points of resistance. Music discovery depends on historical consumption data from music catalogs. Music distribution depends on API access and direct deals with different DSPs. Music promotion depends even more on access to multi-platform data and operational interfaces.

After thinking it through, it seems that playlist traffic growth may be one of the few areas that can be optimized without relying on APIs or closed-loop integrations with each platform.

A colleague once asked me whether distributors will disappear in the long run. I still stand by my original judgment: unless the cost of distribution can be reduced to zero, it is almost impossible.

#### 我的回应

我读到的核心，是你开始把“Codex 能做什么”从泛化的效率工具，放回到一个真实行业的结构里检验。音乐发行和宣推不是单纯的内容生产问题，它被曲库数据、平台接口、DSP 合作关系、宣推资源和操作权限层层约束。所以这里的关键不是“AI 能不能生成方案”，而是“AI 能不能接触到足够多的真实反馈回路”。

你对 Distributor 的判断也很清晰：只要发行不是零成本，聚合、结算、合规、接口、关系和运营支持就仍然有价值。AI 可能会压低某些环节的人工成本，但不一定会消灭中间层。更可能发生的是：低价值的 distributor 被自动化压缩，高价值的 distributor 变成数据、策略和资源接入的操作系统。

我觉得你找到的“歌单流量增长”是一个很好的切入口，因为它更接近可观察、可实验、可迭代的增长问题。下一步可以把它拆成一个小实验：选一个歌单，定义目标用户、内容定位、更新频率、外部分发渠道和每周增长指标。让 Codex 不直接“接平台”，而是先帮你设计实验、整理素材、生成投放文案、记录数据、复盘假设。这样你不是让 AI 替代发行系统，而是先让它成为增长实验的操作伙伴。

Here are 3 references I’d use for your next deep dive, each mapped to the question in your post.

[Spotify for Artists: Pitching music to playlist editors](https://support.spotify.com/ca-en/artists/article/pitching-music-to-playlist-editors/)
Best for your “歌单流量增长” experiment. It states the official playlist pitching path: use Spotify for Artists, pitch unreleased music, submit at least 7 days before release, and provide detailed info. This is a good baseline for what can be optimized without needing distributor/DSP API access.

[Spotify Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy)
Best for understanding why “用 Codex 接平台数据做闭环” is hard. Pay special attention to sections on artificial manipulation, commercial use, quotas, and AI/model restrictions. It makes your instinct sharper: platform data access is not just a technical problem, but a policy and permission problem.

[Spotify Web API documentation](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api) + [Rate Limits](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/rate-limits)
Best for mapping what is technically possible if you later build a music ops assistant. The API can retrieve metadata, manage playlists, and control playback, but rate limits and endpoint access constraints shape what kind of tool is realistic.

#### My response

What I hear at the center of this post is that you are starting to test “what Codex can do” inside the actual structure of an industry, instead of treating it as a generic efficiency tool. Music distribution and promotion are not simply content-production problems. They are constrained by catalog data, platform interfaces, DSP partnerships, promotional resources, and operational permissions. So the real question is not “Can AI generate a plan?” but “Can AI access enough real feedback loops to improve the system?”

Your judgment about distributors is also clear: as long as distribution is not free, aggregation, settlement, compliance, interfaces, relationships, and operational support still have value. AI may reduce labor costs in some parts of the chain, but that does not necessarily eliminate the middle layer. A more likely outcome is that low-value distributors get compressed by automation, while high-value distributors become operating systems for data, strategy, and resource access.

I think “playlist traffic growth” is a strong entry point because it is closer to an observable, testable, iterative growth problem. The next step could be a small experiment: choose one playlist, define the target audience, content positioning, update rhythm, external distribution channels, and weekly growth metrics. Codex does not need direct platform access at first. It can help design the experiment, organize materials, generate promotional copy, record data, and review hypotheses. In that mode, AI is not replacing the distribution system yet; it is becoming an operating partner for growth experiments.
